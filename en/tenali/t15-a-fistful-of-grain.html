A Fistful of Grain — 1000 Gold Coins for a Single Seed

🌾
Tenali Rama Stories Vijayanagara Empire Story of Wit
తెలుగుEnglishहिन्दी
📖 Reading time: ~5 min 👶 Age: 8+ 📂 Series: Tenali Stories ✍️ By: RayalSabha 📅 Published: 2026-10-25
Story Summary

"If you cannot give one fistful of grain… you must pay a thousand gold coins!" In Vijayanagara, a learned woman named Vidyullata put up a board offering a thousand gold coins to anyone who could out-think her. Scholar after scholar failed. Then a humble firewood seller, with just two words — 'a fistful' — led her all the way to the royal court. The seller was none other than Tenali Ramakrishna in disguise. A story about why cleverness needs humility.

01

Vidyullata's Pride — A Challenge Worth 1000 Gold Coins

Vijayanagara

In Vijayanagara there lived a learned woman named Vidyullata.

She was accomplished in learning, poetry and music alike — but her pride in her own intelligence had crossed every limit.

02

The Board at Her Door — No One Could Beat Her

Vidyullata's House

She had a board put up in front of her house: "A prize of one thousand gold coins to whoever defeats my intelligence!"

Many scholars came to debate her. Not one of them could defeat her.

03

The Firewood Seller's Bargain — One Fistful of Grain

Morning

One morning a firewood seller passed by her house, calling out his wares.

🪵 Firewood Seller
"Firewood, madam… good firewood!"
📚 Vidyullata (stepping out, irritated)
"How much for all your firewood?"
🪵 Firewood Seller (calmly)
"I want no money, madam. Just give me one fistful of grain — and all this firewood is yours."
📚 Vidyullata (laughing)
"Such a cheap bargain? Someone like you hasn't the wit to bargain with me."

Saying this proudly, she went inside and brought out a handful of grain.

📚 Vidyullata
"Here! This is what you asked for, isn't it?"
04

What Did 'One Fistful of Grain' Really Mean?

Vidyullata's House

But the seller did not take the grain.

🪵 Firewood Seller
"Madam… I asked for just one fistful of grain. Do you understand?"

Vidyullata grew angry.

📚 Vidyullata (proudly)
"Do you think I don't even know what a fistful of grain is? I am the cleverest woman in this whole region!"

The seller smiled.

🪵 Firewood Seller
"What I asked for is a single grain big enough to fill my hand. Give me just that one."

Vidyullata was stunned. No such grain existed anywhere. In the end, the two of them went to the royal court.

05

The Debate in Court — Who Cheated Whom?

The Royal Court

In the court, Vidyullata argued first.

📚 Vidyullata
"My lord! This man has cheated me. He set this condition knowing full well that no grain big enough to fill a hand exists anywhere!"
🪵 Firewood Seller (calmly)
"My lord, I hid nothing. I stated my condition beforehand. It was she who agreed without thinking."
📚 Vidyullata (cutting in at once)
"My lord! He said 'a fistful of grain'. Ordinarily that means a fistful of grain! Now he twists his words and says a single seed that fills a hand!"
🪵 Firewood Seller
"When striking a bargain, it is the buyer's duty to ask the meaning of every word. And you were the one who claimed your intelligence was greater than everyone's!"
06

The King's Verdict — and the Seller Was Tenali Ramakrishna!

The Royal Court

The King listened to both. The condition had been stated beforehand — and it was Vidyullata who accepted it without fully understanding it.

👑 King (giving the verdict)
"Vidyullata! The challenge was yours. And it was you who accepted the condition. So take down your board, and give this man the thousand gold coins."

Vidyullata stood in silence.

📚 Vidyullata
"Who is this seller who defeated me so cleverly?"

He removed his disguise — it was Tenali Ramakrishna!

He taught her that cleverness is not for showing off — that a wise person must also be humble.

Moral of the Story 🌾

Intelligence makes a person great… but pride defeats that very intelligence.

💡 Life Lesson

In any agreement, ask the meaning of every word. It is those who think 'I already know everything' who get caught by the smallest words.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the moral of the Fistful of Grain story?
+

Intelligence makes a person great, but pride defeats it. A wise person must also be humble.

Who was Vidyullata?
+

A learned woman of Vijayanagara skilled in scholarship, poetry and music, who offered a thousand gold coins to anyone who could out-think her.

What was the trick in 'one fistful of grain'?
+

He didn't ask for a handful of grain, but a single grain big enough to fill a hand. No such grain exists, so she could not meet the condition.

Who was the firewood seller?
+

Tenali Ramakrishna in disguise, who set this trap to cure Vidyullata's pride and teach her humility.

Is this story suitable for children?
+

Yes — it teaches children aged 8 and up to listen carefully to words and to stay humble.
