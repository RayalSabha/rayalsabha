Episode T06

The Painter's Arrogance — Where Is the Flaw in the Painting?

🪔
Vijayanagara Kingdom
Krishnadevaraya's Court
Tenali Rama
తెలుగుEnglishहिन्दी
📖 Reading time: ~6 min read 👶 Age: 8+ 📂 Series: Tenali Stories ✍️ By: RayalSabha 📅 Published: July 2026
🪔 About this Story

A boastful painter challenges the royal court — find one flaw in his covered painting and win a hundred gold coins. Every scholar and rival painter fails. But Tenali Ramakrishna doesn't search the canvas for a flaw — he searches the painter's eyes for fear. A single confident glance is enough to unravel a lie that not even art critics could catch.

01

The Boastful Painter

The painter arrived at court with his painting completely covered in red silk. The whole hall watched, curious and tense.

🎨 Painter
"Your Majesty… what I hold is not merely a painting — a wonder no one has ever seen. There is not a single flaw in it. Whoever finds one, wins a hundred gold coins. Whoever fails owes me one!"

The king smiled and agreed. With a dramatic flourish, the painter whipped away the red cloth in one motion. The painting was revealed — and the entire court gasped in wonder.

02

Everyone Fails

One by one, famous painters and scholars examined the painting. But every time, the painter dismissed their words with careless arrogance.

🧑‍🎨 Critic 1
"This light looks a little unnatural…"
🎨 Painter
"Unnatural? That's just how it looks to your eye."
🧑‍🎨 Critic 2
"This tree's proportion isn't quite right…"
🎨 Painter
"Proportions are for accountants… not artists."
🧑‍🎨 Critic 3
"This river flows a bit strangely…"
🎨 Painter (proudly)
"Rivers flow wherever my brush takes them."

Every answer was different — but the arrogance behind it never changed. By evening, another sack of gold coins had landed at his feet.

03

Tenali Enters

The court's doors opened. Tenali walked in slowly. The painter smirked, already mocking him.

🎨 Painter
"Scholars have failed. Painters have failed. Now… will a poet defeat me?"

Tenali said not a single word. He walked straight up and stood before the painting.

04

The Silence

Tenali leaned closer to the painting — studying a bird, a tree, a single fine line, one by one. Whispers spread through the hall: "Maybe even Tenali can't find it…"

Then Tenali slowly turned and looked straight at the painter — a confident glance, as if he'd found something. The painter caught that look, and his smile vanished. Sweat formed on his brow.

As the court's closing hour drew near, a minister and even the king grew visibly restless — but Tenali was stalling deliberately, exactly as his plan required.

🪶 Tenali
"Your Majesty… I have found the flaw."
👑 King
"Really!! Where is the flaw, Tenali!!?"

Clang… clang… the closing bell of the court rang. Tenali spoke firmly.

🪶 Tenali
"Your Majesty… I will reveal it before everyone, tomorrow morning."

And with that, he left. The painter was left with only one thought echoing in his mind — "What flaw did Tenali find?"

05

The Sleepless Night

The painter could not sleep that night. What did Tenali see? Which line? Which color? Which flaw? No matter how much he thought, the fear only grew.

Finally, at midnight, he took his brush and paints and crept secretly into the palace. Making sure no one was around, he approached the painting — from a distance, quietly altering two lines and adding a touch of color.

🎨 Painter (whispering)
"Now… Tenali can prove nothing."

He turned to leave — when a slow clap echoed from the darkness. The painter froze in shock. Tenali stepped out from the shadows, smiling calmly.

🪶 Tenali
"Tomorrow… it will be you who speaks the truth."

And with that, he walked away calmly. The painter stood frozen in fear.

06

The Final Verdict

The next morning in court, the painter appeared confident on the outside — but fear gripped him inside.

🎨 Painter
"Now tell me, Tenali. Where is the flaw?"

Tenali stepped forward, calm and composed.

🪶 Tenali
"Last night… why did you come here to alter this painting?"

Stung with wounded pride, the painter lashed out angrily, refusing to accept the accusation.

🎨 Painter
"I did not come! Prove it!"
🪶 Tenali
"Then… show me your hands."

The painter immediately stretched out his hands.

🎨 Painter
"See? Not a single drop of paint on my hands."

A few in the court laughed. A minister whispered, "Perhaps Tenali has lost this time…" Tenali only smiled, and walked calmly toward the painter.

🪶 Tenali
"You thought washing your hands would hide the truth. But truth hides under the nails too."

In one swift move, Tenali took the painter's hand and turned it — nails facing up. He carried it to the king. Beneath the nails: tiny flecks of blue paint. The entire court fell silent. The painter lowered his head.

07

The Real Twist

The king asked, astonished — "Tenali… so is there truly a flaw in this painting?" A few moments of complete silence followed. Tenali smiled.

🪶 Tenali
"I don't know, Your Majesty. What I searched for was not a flaw in the painting — it was a man's fear that a flaw might be found. That very fear is what brought him back like a thief in the night."

The king smiled — "Magnificent, Tenali!" Tenali turned to face the court one last time, and after a brief pause, spoke.

🪶 Tenali
"Your Majesty… a flawless painting may exist… but a flawless person never does."

The entire court erupted in applause.

🪔 Moral of the Story

A flawless painting may exist —
a flawless person never does. Fear always reveals the truth.

🌿 Life Lesson

The painter wasn't caught by his brush — he was caught by his own fear. Guilt has a way of moving the body even when the mouth stays silent. Honesty, in the end, is the only flaw-proof defense.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions
What is the moral of this story?
+

A flawless painting may exist, but a flawless person never does — fear always reveals the truth a person tries to hide.

How did Tenali find the flaw in the painting?
+

Tenali never actually found a flaw in the painting. He searched instead for the painter's fear — and used that fear itself as his weapon.

How was the painter's scheme exposed?
+

Driven by fear, the painter secretly returned at night to alter the painting. Even after washing his hands, flecks of blue paint left under his nails proved his lie.

Is this story suitable for children?
+

Yes, this story works well for children 8 and up — a suspenseful, gentle lesson about arrogance and honesty.

Who is Tenali Ramakrishna?
+

Tenali Ramakrishna was a court poet of Emperor Krishnadevaraya of the Vijayanagara Empire, and one of his legendary Ashtadiggajas — famous for his wit and humor.
