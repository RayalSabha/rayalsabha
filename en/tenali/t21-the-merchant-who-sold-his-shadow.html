Episode T21

The Merchant Who Sold His Shadow

🌳
Tenali Rama Stories Vijayanagara Empire Funny Moral Story
తెలుగుEnglishहिन्दी
📖 Reading time: ~7 min 👶 Age: 6+ 📂 Series: Tenali Stories ✍️ By: RayalSabha 📅 Published: 2026-10-25
Story Summary

'Save me, Your Majesty! Ramakrishna has brought men with hammers to tear down my house!' — the merchant Kanakayya came running into court with a neem twig still in his mouth. The trouble began the afternoon before, under a tree. Kanakayya put a price on free shade and sold it to Tenali Ramakrishna for a hundred gold coins, promising 'wherever the shade goes, it is yours.' The next morning the sun moved — and the shade fell right on his house! A hilarious tale about what greed earns.

01

Kanakayya's Complaint — With a Neem Twig in His Mouth

The Royal Court

Towel on his shoulder and a neem twig in his mouth, Kanakayya ran in through the golden doors and dropped to his knees on the red carpet. The whole court turned to stare.

💰 Kanakayya
"Save me, Your Majesty! Ramakrishna-garu has brought men with hammers to tear down my house!"
👑 King (staring at the neem twig)
"First take that neem twig out of your mouth, Kanakayya — then make your complaint."
👑 King (half stern, half laughing)
"What's this, Ramakrishna? Have you given up poetry to start knocking down houses?"
🪶 Tenali Rama (innocently shrugging)
"I've done nothing, my lord. His house is standing in the way of my own shade, so I asked for it to be removed. That's all."
👑 King
"Your shade? All right — tell me this shade story from the very beginning."
02

Must One Pay Rent for a Tree's Shade?

Yesterday Afternoon

This whole quarrel actually began yesterday afternoon, under the shade of a tree.

Walking in from the blazing sun, Ramakrishna wiped his sweat, sat on the stone platform in the neem tree's shade, fanned himself with his cloth and sighed with relief.

💰 Kanakayya (mockingly)
"Well, well, Kavi-garu — resting in my tree's shade for free, are we?"
🪶 Tenali Rama (opening just one eye)
"Must one pay rent even for a tree's shade, Kanakayya?"
💰 Kanakayya (greedily rubbing his palms)
"Why bother with petty rent? I'll sell you the shade itself! A hundred gold coins — and this shade is yours for life!"
🧑‍🌾 Villager 1 (scratching his head)
"Selling a tree I can understand — but selling its shade?!"
🧑‍🌾 Villager 2 (whispering)
"Leave him like this and tomorrow he'll be measuring sunshine and weighing the wind to sell them!"
03

The Shade Is Sold for a Hundred Gold Coins

Yesterday Afternoon
💰 Kanakayya (winking)
"Don't think twice, Kavi-garu — you won't find such a big shade this cheap anywhere in all Vijayanagara!"

Ramakrishna slowly looked at the shade, back over his shoulder, then up at the sun — a mysterious smile on his face.

🪶 Tenali Rama (laughing heartily)
"True — I've never in my life seen a merchant as clever as you."

Ramakrishna counted the gold coins into Kanakayya's palm. Kanakayya bit each coin to check it.

🪶 Tenali Rama (loud enough for all to hear)
"Everyone, bear witness! From now on this shade is mine. Wherever it goes, it remains mine."
💰 Kanakayya (holding his belly, laughing)
"Without a doubt! Wherever the shade goes, it's yours — ha ha ha!"
💰 Kanakayya (kissing the bag)
"A hundred gold coins for shade! At this rate, tomorrow I'll sell the moonlight too!"
04

Next Morning — The Shade Falls on Kanakayya's House!

Next Morning

Early the next morning, a brand-new problem landed on Kanakayya's house.

Kanakayya sat on his porch brushing his teeth with a neem twig, humming a tune. Then came heavy footsteps — Ramakrishna arrived with five workers carrying hammers and crowbars.

🪶 Tenali Rama (pointing at the wall)
"Start with this wall."
💰 Kanakayya (jumping up, twig in mouth)
"Oh no, oh no! Kavi-garu, why are you knocking down my house?"
🪶 Tenali Rama (calmly)
"Look, Kanakayya — the sun has moved. The shade I bought yesterday is now lying right on top of your house."
💰 Kanakayya (dumbfounded)
"The shade? … On my house? … What are you talking about, Kavi-garu?"
🪶 Tenali Rama
"Your house is sitting on my shade. If someone else's things are on my property, I have the right to have them removed, don't I?"
💰 Kanakayya (arms outstretched, pleading)
"Sir, what you bought was the shade — not my house!"
🪶 Tenali Rama (raising one eyebrow)
"Yesterday, in front of everyone, did you or did you not say, 'Wherever the shade goes, it is yours'?"
💰 Kanakayya (in a small voice)
"I did."
🪶 Tenali Rama (turning to the workers)
"You heard him. Begin."
💰 Kanakayya (hugging the wall)
"No, no! My grandfather built this wall!"
🪶 Tenali Rama (innocently)
"Then your grandfather built it right in the way of my shade."
💰 Kanakayya (clutching his head)
"Oh God! I sold the shade for a hundred coins and put my own house on the chopping block!"
05

The Verdict — Buying Back the Shade Costs a Thousand Coins!

The Royal Court

Hearing the story, the King leaned back on his throne, holding his belly with laughter. The whole court laughed.

👑 King (laughing)
"Kanakayya, you showed such cleverness selling the shade. Didn't you think about what happens if the buyer is cleverer than you?"
💰 Kanakayya (hands folded)
"I made a mistake, Your Majesty! Please give me back my shade."
🪶 Tenali Rama (bowing slightly)
"Certainly. I'm happy to sell back the shade I bought."
💰 Kanakayya (hopefully)
"For how much, Kavi-garu?"
🪶 Tenali Rama (as if counting on his fingers)
"One thousand gold coins."
💰 Kanakayya (mouth falling open)
"A thousand?! I sold it for just a hundred!"
🪶 Tenali Rama (with a smile)
"Back then it was just a tree's shade. Now there's a whole house under it — doesn't that raise the property's value?"

The entire court burst out laughing.

👑 King (gravely)
"Kanakayya, you put a price on shade that God gives everyone for free. This is the reward of greed."
👑 King
"Pay the price Ramakrishna asks and buy back your shade. Otherwise, your house stays in his shade."

Trembling, Kanakayya handed the heavy bag of coins to Ramakrishna.

🪶 Tenali Rama
"My lord, let us use this money to plant trees all along the market street. Anyone may sit in their shade for free — Kanakayya included."
🪶 Tenali Rama (looking at Kanakayya)
"He asked for money to sit in a tree's shade, my lord. Now he's had to pay for the shade over his own house."
👑 King (pointing at the neem twig, laughing)
"Now go home and finish brushing those teeth, Kanakayya!"

Looking at the neem twig in his hand, a red-faced Kanakayya ran out through the golden doors as the court rang with laughter.

Moral of the Story 🌳

Put a price on what God gives everyone for free, and greed will drown you in the end.

💡 Life Lesson

When making a deal, think through every meaning of your own words. Cleverness used to cheat others becomes our own trap the moment we meet someone cleverer.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the moral of The Merchant Who Sold His Shadow?
+

Greed that puts a price on what is free for everyone ends up drowning us.

Why were they about to knock down Kanakayya's house?
+

Kanakayya had sold the shade promising 'wherever it goes, it's yours'. Next morning the sun moved and the shade fell on his house — so Ramakrishna claimed the right to remove whatever was blocking his shade.

For how much did Ramakrishna sell the shade back?
+

For a thousand gold coins — since a house now stood under it, 'the property value had gone up'. He then suggested using the money to plant shady trees for everyone along the market street.

Is this story suitable for children?
+

Yes — a very funny story for children aged 6 and up that also teaches about greed and keeping one's word.
